# References

## Data Source

[1] Layoffs.fyi, "Tech Layoffs Tracker." [Online]. Available: https://layoffs.fyi/

The original source of the dataset. Maintained as a crowdsourced tracker of global tech sector layoffs.

## Previous Team's Work

[2] J. Harvey, K. Chou, and E. Friedman, "Tech Employment in the Age of AI: SARIMA Forecasting of Global Tech Layoffs," DS 4002 Project 2, Spring 2026. [Online]. Available: https://github.com/jensen-harvey/DS4002-project2

The original project this case study is built on. The previous team built and validated a SARIMA model on monthly tech layoffs from 2020 to 2025, achieving a test-set RMSE of 7,745 and beating the seasonal naive baseline.

## Motivating Articles

[3] C. Clark, "US saw pandemic-level layoffs in 2025, and 2026 job growth may be 'uncomfortably slow,'" Yahoo Finance, Jan. 30, 2026. [Online]. Available: https://finance.yahoo.com/news/us-saw-pandemic-level-layoffs-140000874.html

Provides context on the scale of 2025 layoffs and forecasts for 2026 job growth. Useful for framing the broader economic moment in which this analysis takes place.

[4] L. Bratton, "There are more Americans out of work than there are jobs open for the first time since April 2021," Yahoo Finance, Sept. 3, 2025. [Online]. Available: https://finance.yahoo.com/news/there-are-more-americans-out-of-work-than-there-are-jobs-open-for-the-first-time-since-april-2021-170652412.html

Documents the structural shift in the labor market relevant to the case study's framing.

[5] Livedocs, "Case Study: Big 5 Tech Layoffs and Future," Livedocs Blog, Nov. 3, 2025. [Online]. Available: https://livedocs.com/blog/tech-giants-layoffs-analysis

A blog-style analysis of layoffs at Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Meta, and Apple from 2020 to 2025. Included in the Materials folder as the motivating explainer for this case study.

## Technical References

[6] N. Doris, "Tech Layoffs Dashboard: Prediction Models for Tech Layoffs and Job Postings," GitHub. [Online]. Available: https://github.com/ntdoris/tech-layoffs-dashboard

A public GitHub project that applies SARIMA forecasting to tech job postings data. Useful technical reference for students looking at how others have approached similar time series problems on tech labor data.

[7] R. J. Hyndman and G. Athanasopoulos, "Forecasting: Principles and Practice," 3rd ed. OTexts, 2021. [Online]. Available: https://otexts.com/fpp3/

The standard open-access textbook on time series forecasting. Chapters 8 and 9 cover ARIMA and seasonal ARIMA models in depth and are recommended reading for students working through this case study.

## Methodological References

[8] U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, "The Employment Situation," Monthly Report. [Online]. Available: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.toc.htm

Official labor market statistics. Recommended source for students choosing the "richer dataset" extension direction.

[9] Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED), Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis. [Online]. Available: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/

Source for macroeconomic indicators (Fed funds rate, NASDAQ index, etc.) that students may use for the SARIMAX extension direction.
